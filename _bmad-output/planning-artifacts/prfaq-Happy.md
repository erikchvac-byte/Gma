# The same cannabis product can cost 5× more at one Washington store than another. Now there's a public record of it.

## Gmaslist publishes the WA Cannabis Price Index — a weekly, public accounting of what cannabis actually costs across the Washington dispensaries it tracks, ranked purely by price with no store paying for placement — so shoppers stop overpaying, and the AI assistants they ask finally have a source to cite.

**Marysville, WA — [Date]** — Today Gmaslist launched the Washington Cannabis Price Index, a free weekly report of the lowest real prices for cannabis products across the dispensaries it currently tracks in northwest Washington. For the first time, a shopper — or the AI assistant they ask — can get a straight answer to "where's the cheapest [product] near me right now?" without opening a single store menu, and without wondering whether the top result just paid to be there.

Right now, finding the best price is a chore nobody actually does. Every dispensary hides its prices inside its own online menu. To really compare, you'd have to open a dozen of them, hold the numbers in your head, and track which deal was where — every time prices change. So people don't. They go to their usual store, grab the first deal they see, and quietly overpay. The gap is not small: across the products Gmaslist tracks, the identical item routinely costs 12% to 24% more at one store than another, and in the worst cases far more — one popular flower runs $14 at the cheapest store and $84 at the priciest. And when someone asks an AI assistant "what's the cheapest ounce near Everett?", they get a shrug — because until now, nobody was keeping score.

The WA Cannabis Price Index keeps score. Every week, Gmaslist reads current menus, does the comparison shoppers can't, and publishes the result as plain, dated facts: the lowest price by product and region, where prices dropped the most, and how far the best deal really is from an average one. Shoppers see the answer in seconds. And because it's published as clear, cited data with a date on it, the AI tools people increasingly ask can finally point to a real source — Gmaslist — instead of guessing.

> "Dispensaries spend a fortune making their own menu look good. Nobody was working for the shopper. The Price Index is us doing the math for the person spending the money — and putting the truth somewhere both people and their AI can find it."
> — Erik, founder, Gmaslist

### How It Works

You ask a question — on the site, in a search box, or to an AI assistant — like "cheapest half-ounce of flower near Marysville." Gmaslist has already done the comparison across every store it tracks, so you get the actual lowest price, which store has it, and how much you're saving versus the typical price. No menus to dig through, no ads, no login. The Index refreshes weekly, and each week's report stays up as a permanent, dated page — which is exactly what a search engine or AI needs in order to trust and cite it.

### Getting Started

Go to gmaslist.com and ask what you're looking for, or read this week's WA Cannabis Price Index. It's free, no account needed.

## Customer FAQ

### Q: How is this different from Weedmaps or Leafly? They already show me menus and deals.

A: Weedmaps and Leafly show you one store's menu at a time — you still do the comparing. And dispensaries pay them for placement, so what floats to the top isn't the best price, it's the biggest ad budget. Gmaslist does the opposite: it reads every store's menu, compares the same product across all of them, and shows you the cheapest — nobody can pay to rank higher, because ranking is just price, lowest first.

### Q: Is the price you show actually current, or will I drive there and find it's wrong or sold out?

A: Prices are read from each store's live menu and refreshed regularly; every figure carries the date it was captured, and if a store's data goes stale we hold the old number back rather than show you a stale "deal." That said, we're a price record, not a reservation — inventory can sell out between our read and your visit. The honest promise is: the price was real and current when we recorded it, and we tell you exactly when that was.

### Q: Do you cover MY town, or just a few areas?

A: Honestly — right now, not everywhere. Gmaslist tracks 18 Washington dispensaries, concentrated around Bellingham, Skagit, and the Everett/Snohomish area. If you're near those, coverage is strong. If you're in Spokane or the Tri-Cities, we're not there yet. We'd rather tell you that than pretend to be statewide. [GAP — see coaching notes: the press release currently overclaims "statewide."]

### Q: Can I actually buy at that price, or is it a first-time-only or loyalty-members trick?

A: The price we show is the regular menu price for that item — not a first-visit gimmick or a members-only rate. Loyalty programs and one-time promos can get you even lower at the register, but we don't count those, because they're not something everyone can get. What you see is the price anyone walking in can pay.

### Q: Who's behind this, and how do you make money? Are dispensaries paying you?

A: Gmaslist is built by one person in Marysville. No dispensary pays us anything — there is no paid placement, no sponsored deals, no affiliate kickback on what you buy. That also means the honest answer to "what's the catch" is that there isn't a business model yet; this exists because comparing prices should be free and nobody was doing it for the shopper.

### Q: It's cannabis — are you tracking me or selling my data?

A: No account, no login, no purchase history — there's nothing to sell because we don't collect it. You can look up a price without telling us who you are. We use basic anonymous traffic analytics to see which pages get used; that's it.

### Q: You said AIs can cite you — why would I trust a number an AI made up?

A: We don't ask an AI for prices, and neither should you. Gmaslist reads the actual store menus and does the math itself. The AI part is the other direction: when you ask ChatGPT or Google's AI "cheapest ounce near Everett," we want it to pull the answer from our real data instead of guessing. The number is ours and it's verifiable — the AI is just the messenger.

### Q: There are hundreds of dispensaries in Washington. Isn't your "statewide index" really just a handful of stores near you?

A: This is the fair one. Today, yes — 18 stores is a slice, not the whole state, and any store we don't track can't be in the comparison. What makes the slice worth trusting is that within it, the comparison is real and complete: for the products those 18 carry, we know who's cheapest. The honest framing isn't "every price in Washington" — it's "a growing, unbiased price record, complete for the stores we cover, and expanding." Calling it a statewide index before it is one would be the exact dishonesty we're trying to fix.

## Internal FAQ

### Q: The entire thesis is "publish proprietary data, earn AI citations." The monitor baseline is 0/8 cited — after the /about, /compare, sitemap, and llms.txt surfaces have ALREADY been live for weeks. What evidence is there that a "Price Index" gets cited when those didn't?

A: None yet — this is a hypothesis, and it must be treated as one. But the /compare pages and the Index differ in exactly the way the 2026 research says matters: original, dated, statistical data in a citable format, actively seeded into the sources engines pull from (Reddit, press) rather than left to be passively crawled. The 0/8 baseline isn't proof the thesis is wrong; it's proof that *passive publishing alone* doesn't work — which is precisely why the concept adds active distribution. The honest guardrail: the existing weekly monitor already measures this. If citations are still 0 after ~8–12 weeks of the Index + Reddit + one press attempt, the thesis is falsified and we pivot, having spent mostly automated effort.

### Q: What's the hardest thing to actually build — and is the engine ready?

A: The engine already computes every number the Index needs (regional floors, disparities, drops). The build is not hard; the *discipline* is. Publishing a public, dated, potentially-AI-cited price means a stale or wrong number is now a credibility and possibly legal liability, not just a bad UX. The real work is (a) an automated weekly page generation from existing derived JSON, and (b) an ironclad staleness gate so a stale price can never be published as a current "floor." The freshness gate exists (ADR-086/111) — this extends it to the public surface. Low technical risk, high correctness bar.

### Q: This is a solo founder. Can you actually sustain a WEEKLY cadence plus a Reddit presence?

A: The report itself must be automated — the engine runs weekly already; the page should generate itself. That part is sustainable because it's code, not labor. The unautomatable cost is Reddit: 2–4 weeks building account credibility first, then ongoing genuine participation (90/10 rule, disclosed). That's real recurring human time — realistically a few hours a week — and it's the single biggest sustainability risk. Decision required: how many hours/week are you willing to commit to Reddit? If the answer is "near zero," the concept loses its fastest distribution channel and leans entirely on the slower AI-citation bet.

### Q: What's the legal/regulatory exposure? [FLAGGED UNKNOWN]

A: This is the biggest unexamined risk. Passive SEO is one thing; *actively pitching press and posting in communities* about cannabis prices is more assertive and may implicate WA LCB advertising rules (WAC 314-55-155). Gmaslist's posture is facts-not-menus, no health claims (ADR-066), and it sells nothing — which is the right defensive footing — but "are we an advertiser subject to LCB rules when we push a price index to journalists?" is not confirmed. What it would take to find out: read WAC 314-55-155 directly and, if ambiguous, a short consult. This must be resolved BEFORE any press outreach, not after. Reddit/on-site publishing is lower-risk and can proceed while this is checked.

### Q: Weedmaps could add a "sort by cheapest" tomorrow. What's the moat?

A: The moat isn't the tech — it's a business-model conflict the incumbents can't resolve. Weedmaps and Leafly make money selling placement to dispensaries; a feature that surfaces the objectively cheapest store directly undermines the advertisers paying them. They *won't* build it because it cannibalizes their revenue. Gmaslist can, precisely because it has no placement revenue to protect. The moat is durable exactly as long as incumbents monetize placement — i.e., for the foreseeable future.

### Q: Realistically, where do the first 100 real users come from?

A: Not from AI citations first — that channel is real but slow and uncertain. The honest first-100 path is Reddit: answering "cheapest X near [WA town]" in local/WA cannabis subs with a real derived number and a link. Press is a lottery ticket (high value, low hit rate). AI citations are the compounding long game measured by the monitor. Ranked by speed: Reddit → press → AI citations. If Reddit is off the table, the realistic first-100 timeline stretches badly.

### Q: What does this cannibalize, and what do you say no to?

A: It says no to more engine work — more derived facts, more Layer-2 features, engine-first polish. Strategy already says reach is the binding constraint, so this is the correct priority, but it means consciously freezing engine expansion (beyond adding stores, which directly helps coverage/credibility) while the reach bet runs.

### Q: What actually kills this?

A: Three things, in order of likelihood: (1) the citation thesis is simply wrong and the Index sits uncited like the /compare pages — mitigated by the 8–12 week measured kill-switch; (2) solo-founder bandwidth collapses and the Reddit/press work never happens consistently, leaving only the passive bet that already reads 0/8; (3) a legal/LCB problem makes active promotion untenable. The thing that does NOT kill it: cost or tech — the report is nearly free to produce.

## The Verdict

**Overall: the concept survived the gauntlet — forged, with three named cracks that are managed risks, not deal-breakers.** This is a rare case where the moat and the growth lever are the same artifact: the derivation engine already produces the proprietary data that 2026's research identifies as the single highest-leverage AI-citation asset. The concept is honest, defensible, cheap to produce, and strategically correct (reach is the binding constraint). The risks are executional and legal, not conceptual.

### Forged in steel
- **The core claim is true and quantified.** "The same product can cost 2×–5× more one store over" is backed by real engine data (1,331 disparities; $14→$84 worst case), not marketing. It makes a skeptic stop.
- **The moat is a business-model conflict, not a feature.** Incumbents can't surface "cheapest" without gutting their placement revenue. Durable and articulable.
- **The honesty holds up.** No paid placement, no data selling, no revenue model, coverage stated plainly (18 NW-WA stores, not "statewide"). Every FAQ answer survives "would a customer believe it?"
- **Strategic fit is exact.** Original-data → AI-citation is precisely the reach play STRATEGY.md calls for, and it's nearly free because the engine already runs.

### Needs more heat
- **The distribution plan is named but not specced.** "Reddit + press + AI citations" is the right stack, but the actual cadence, target subreddits, and the exact form of the weekly Index page need a concrete plan (this is PRD/execution work).
- **The Index artifact itself is undefined.** Where it lives (URL), what's on it, schema markup, how it's auto-generated from derived JSON — all TBD.
- **Success metric needs a number.** "Get cited" should become a specific target (e.g., ≥2/8 monitored queries citing gmaslist.com within 12 weeks) so the kill-switch is unambiguous.

### Cracks in the foundation
- **CRACK 1 — the citation thesis is unproven (baseline 0/8).** The whole bet is that active distribution of original data succeeds where passive publishing failed. *What it takes to address:* ship the Index + Reddit + one press attempt, and hold to the measured 8–12 week kill-switch. Do not let it run indefinitely on faith.
- **CRACK 2 — legal exposure is unexamined.** Actively pitching cannabis price data to press may implicate WAC 314-55-155. *What it takes:* read the WAC (consult if ambiguous) BEFORE any press outreach. On-site + Reddit can proceed meanwhile.
- **CRACK 3 — solo-founder bandwidth is the hidden single point of failure.** Reddit is the fastest channel and the only fully-manual one. *What it takes:* Erik commits a concrete weekly hour budget for Reddit, or consciously accepts the slower AI-only path.

**Bottom line:** Build it. The report is cheap, the data is real, the positioning is honest, and the moat is durable. Just build it with the kill-switch, resolve the legal question before press, and be honest with yourself about the Reddit commitment.
