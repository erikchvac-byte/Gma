# Addendum — Gma's Helper B2B Data Licensing Brief

Supporting depth for downstream work (PRD / validation / go-to-market). Not required reading for the
brief itself.

## Why dispensaries as the primary buyer (decision rationale)

Considered three buyer segments from the market research; led with dispensaries. Reasoning:

| Buyer | Pain intensity | Willingness to pay | Reachability (solo founder) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dispensaries | High — price war, direct margin impact | Unvalidated — suggestive by analogy, not proven | High — local, many, Gma's already knows the WA store set | Primary |
| Brands / producers | Medium-high — shelf visibility matters | Likely higher per-deal, also unvalidated | Low — fewer, gatekept, need relationships | Secondary / later |
| Analysts / media / investors | Low-medium — nice-to-have | Variable | Medium — but tiny volume | Secondary / opportunistic |

Evidence and its limit: Leafbuyer (the closest cannabis-deal aggregator) abandoned its consumer
product and now earns ~$6.47M/yr selling SMS/loyalty/marketing SaaS to dispensaries. This shows stores
will pay for store-facing retention/marketing tools — suggestive that a paid store tool can work — but
it is NOT proof of demand for competitive price intelligence, which is a different product with a
different budget and buyer. Willingness-to-pay for this specific product remains the #1 unknown and
the reason the brief is framed validate-first.

## Competitive comparable — Headset (and why Gma's is a different altitude)

Headset is the dominant cannabis B2B data/analytics company. It sells aggregate POS-panel market
analytics (category trends, market share, brand performance). That is a different product from what
Gma's has:
- Headset: "how is the market moving" — panel-based, aggregate, retrospective.
- Gma's: "what is this specific store near you advertising on the shelf right now, and how deep is the
  deal" — public-menu ground truth, store-level, continuously refreshed, freshness-stamped.

Gma's is not trying to beat Headset at market analytics. It owns a narrower, more operationally
actionable slice: advertised shelf price and deal depth at named WA stores, current. A store setting
next week's flower price cares more about the latter.

## Pricing thoughts (price point to validate; target locked)

- Success target (locked): ~$1–2K/month MRR — a real part-time income, the bar that calls the WA
  model validated.
- The strategic point of B2B over ads: meaningful MRR at low buyer counts. A handful of dispensary
  subscribers at a modest monthly price beats ~100k pageviews of cannabis display ads.
- Working math to the target (per-store price still to be tested in buyer conversations): a
  local-market subscription in the low-hundreds/month range means ~10–20 stores clears ~$1–2K/month;
  the ad model needs vastly more scale for the same.
- The open item is the exact per-store price, not the target — the success criterion is getting a
  real store to name a price it would pay.

## Data-rights / legal posture (expanded)

- The exposure: scraping Dutchie/store menus and commercially reselling carries ToS/IP risk that is
  materially higher for a paid B2B product than for a free consumer site.
- Mitigation posture in the brief: license derived facts (medians, comparisons, deal-depth, trends),
  not raw menu redistribution. Derived analysis is both more valuable and a stronger legal position
  than verbatim re-publication.
- To resolve before selling: (1) a real read on the legal footing (ideally a lawyer familiar with
  data-scraping/ToS and WA law); (2) whether any sources should be partnered/licensed rather than
  scraped; (3) how to phrase what is sold (intelligence/analysis, not "menu data").
- This is the gating risk: the brief recommends validate-first — do buyer discovery and legal clarity
  in parallel before building the productized offering.

## Relationship to the consumer product and overall strategy

- From the research: grassroots/word-of-mouth grows the consumer audience (slow, trust-building, right
  for the market); ads are scale-gated and a late stream. B2B data licensing is the recommended first
  revenue because it pays before scale and doesn't touch consumer neutrality.
- Same pipeline, two products: the consumer site and the B2B feed are powered by one ingest/derivation
  engine. Expanding store coverage improves both simultaneously — a genuine compounding effect.
- Neutrality guardrail: B2B buyers must never be able to influence what the consumer site shows.
  Selling intelligence about the market is fine; selling placement in the consumer view is the
  pay-to-play trap that would destroy Gma's only moat.

## Open questions carried forward

- Precise data-rights legal footing (gating).
- Real dispensary willingness-to-pay and the exact per-store figure/packaging (the #1 unknown; needs
  a paid pilot or LOI, not a verbal yes). Income target is set at ~$1–2K/month MRR; the per-store
  price that reaches it is what's untested.
- Value-over-DIY: is Gma's enough better than a store checking public menus itself / tasking a VA?
- Coverage density: which local WA cluster has enough competitor coverage to be sellable first
  (likely the existing North Puget Sound footprint)?
- Dutchie concentration: fallback if Dutchie changes access or objects (source + product both depend on it).
- Brand-optics: how to serve dispensaries without eroding consumer-side trust.
- Delivery form-factor: dashboard vs. feed vs. both; how light can v1 be.
- Whether a brand or analyst pilot could be an easier/faster first dollar than expected.
